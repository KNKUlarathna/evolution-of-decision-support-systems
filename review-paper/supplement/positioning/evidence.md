# Prior-review positioning and appraisal

Assessment date: 21 October 2026. Four PDFs were assessed for relevant methods, conceptual structure, historical claims, and limitations. Two additional candidates remain abstract-only or metadata-only; they must not be counted as full-text-assessed evidence. This is targeted related-work assessment, not exhaustive novelty verification.

## Main finding for the new manuscript

Neither technological/organizational coevolution nor feedback is a newly discovered premise. Shim et al. already discuss both technology responding to complex organizational decision requirements and communication technology enabling more complex organizational arrangements. Budianto et al. already discuss data-centric feedback, hidden feedback loops, and technical/coordination complexity. Eze et al. already combine information-processing theory, data quality, architecture, human oversight, and an iterative DSS process model.

The defensible contribution is a **conditional, evidence-typed integrative explanation** connecting these strands: specify distinct variables (organizational interdependence/differentiation/equivocality, data volume/variety, processing/coordination capacity, decision enactment), trace each directional link separately, distinguish reinforcing from balancing mechanisms, and mark which links are theory, historical interpretation, field evidence, simulation, or untested propositions. Present a mechanism framework and bounded research agenda, not an empirically established universal law or a first-ever feedback model.

## Source-level extraction

### Shim2002 — Shim et al., *Past, Present, and Future of Decision Support Technology*

- **Access and design:** full PDF, 16 pages, published pp. 111–126. Narrative synthesis based on a panel discussion, not a systematic causal study. Metadata title, volume, pages checked in PDF; DOI verified against a fresh Crossref title query: `10.1016/S0167-9236(01)00139-7`.
- **p. 111 (PDF p. 1), abstract:** explicitly attributes DSS evolution to technological **and organizational** developments and tracks individual, group, virtual-team, and interorganizational support. Supports a multicausal historical framing; it does not isolate effects.
- **p. 113 (PDF p. 3):** ill-defined DSS information needs and ill-structured decision processes motivate flexible databases/query languages/modeling. It dates group support emergence approximately to 1985, while describing coexistence with other approaches.
- **p. 113:** anticipates that Internet/Web/telecommunications will yield organizations that are more global, complex and connected, including integrated global supply chains. This is prior articulation of the *technology enables organizational complexity* direction, but the passage is prospective, not a longitudinal effect estimate.
- **p. 116 (PDF p. 6):** Web warehouses face more simultaneous users and increased data volumes while needing consistent response times; e-commerce/CRM increase demand for customer-transaction analysis. This supports an explicit link from organizational activity/data use to processing and architectural requirements.
- **p. 116:** cross-boundary networks accompany movement from individual to team and diverse multi-firm decisions. It presents co-occurrence and interpretation; it does not establish a clean causal ordering.
- **p. 122 (PDF p. 12):** Web support enables virtual-team structures, virtual organizations and remote business partnerships. Suggests the reverse enabling link; again a historical/prescriptive synthesis rather than a causal identification study.
- **Appraisal:** strong historical anchor and novelty boundary. Use to explain that reciprocity predates current AI. Do not treat all its forecasts (mobile, ubiquitous support, ASP delivery) as verified outcomes.

### ArnottPervan2008 — Arnott and Pervan, *Eight Key Issues for the Decision Support Systems Discipline*

- **Access and design:** full PDF, published pp. 657–672. Content analysis of 1093 DSS research articles in 14 journals, 1990–2004. This is evidence about a research field, not an organizational-adoption dataset.
- **p. 659 (PDF p. 3):** describes electronic selection followed by manual journal-content checks, full-text verification, example-article coding, coder calibration, and review of research-assistant coding. It also explains difficulties in coding organizational DSS across different categories. This is useful contrast to category-based chronological claims and illustrates transparent extraction.
- **p. 662 (PDF p. 6):** distinguishes reference theory merely cited in an introduction from reference theory used in research design and interpretation. Reports 47.8% of sampled papers lacking the latter form of judgment/decision-making grounding. Use only with its 1990–2004 sample boundary; do not generalize to all present research.
- **p. 667 (PDF p. 11), Table 11:** identifies professional relevance, theory, and field fragmentation as continuing challenges and recommends more context-sensitive case-study work.
- **Appraisal:** supports grounding the new paper in a theory actually used to derive/test mechanisms, rather than name-checking organizational information-processing theory. Does not test complexity–data feedback itself and should not be misrepresented as doing so.

### Budianto2026 — Budianto et al., *The Evolution of DSS to Strategic AI*

- **Access and metadata:** full 12-page PDF, published pp. 8905–8916. Title, authors, DOI, dates, volume/issue checked against Crossref and PDF. CC BY 4.0 explicitly stated in PDF footers and Crossref record.
- **p. 8905, abstract and introduction:** frames DSS-to-Strategic-AI evolution as architectural/organizational/business-value transformation. The abstract claims systematic coverage and 85 included papers. That is the authors' report, not an independently verified fact about their screening.
- **pp. 8906–8907, methods:** reports Scopus/WoS/IEEE Xplore searches, 540 initial records, 310 screened, 125 full-text assessed, 85 included. The visible paper does not provide an 85-item included-study list, record-level screening decisions or a study-quality extraction dataset. Its bibliography ends at [38]. This limits independent reconstruction; it is **not an allegation that the study count was fabricated**, because an included-study list could exist outside the visible document.
- **p. 8907, Table 2:** evolutionary phase table links DSS, big data, digital transformation, AI strategy and generative AI to architectures and business value. This is close structural prior art to a stage-comparison review.
- **pp. 8909–8910:** cognitive, orchestration, execution, and feedback/learning layers; continuous monitoring, drift, retraining, organizational learning, and human feedback. Thus a new paper cannot claim that prior reviews omit feedback altogether.
- **pp. 8911–8912, §§3.7–3.8:** discusses hidden feedback loops where model outputs influence later training data, data dependencies, coordination/configuration complexity, data-centric development and a data-improvement cycle. This is very close to the reverse-loop component of the proposed manuscript.
- **p. 8914, conclusion:** calls for empirical tests of its proposed architecture/business-value framework. Its propositions about architectural autonomy and strategic value should be treated as conceptual, not established causal effects.
- **Appraisal:** use as closely related synthesis and compare organizing mechanisms directly. Do not import unsupported universal claims such as exponential debt with autonomy, inevitable obsolescence of conventional DSS, or guaranteed strategic advantage. Anchor those issues to stronger primary evidence where available.

### Eze2026 — Eze et al., *DSS: Evolution, Architectures, Applications, and Future Directions*

- **Access and metadata:** full PDF, published pp. 19–31; Crossref and PDF title-page verification. CC BY 4.0 explicitly stated on final page and in Crossref record.
- **pp. 20–21, §2:** describes an integrative review rather than a PRISMA review/meta-analysis, targeted database searches, extraction dimensions, and context-dependent interpretation. The accessible article reports 40 studies; no source-by-source selection log is attached to the PDF. Treat as the authors' review description, not an independently reproduced search.
- **p. 21, §3.1 and Table 1:** conventional five-way DSS taxonomy and contemporary hybrid systems. No basis for saying data/model/knowledge/communication approaches universally replace one another.
- **p. 22, §3.2:** explicitly uses information-processing, sociotechnical, design-science and organizational theories. Simply adding organizational complexity to a review is therefore not by itself distinctive.
- **p. 22, §4.1:** cloud/distribution/microservice and model-serving architectures; narrative links between architecture and coordination/scalability, not isolated causal estimates.
- **p. 23, Table 2:** retains numerical accuracy/time-reduction/satisfaction comparisons across broad DSS classes without explaining common datasets, tasks or uncertainty. The adjacent §4.2 says that heterogeneous outcomes should not be directly compared and that an earlier Table 3 comparison was unsupported. This internal tension means the new review should **not use those class-level numerical values as effect evidence**. It is an appraisal limitation, not an allegation about author intent.
- **pp. 24–25:** data heterogeneity, integration, governance and lifecycle burden, human and regulatory requirements. Closely related scope to organizational complexity and data growth.
- **pp. 25–27, §6.1:** proposed divergent–convergent process model integrates model/data/knowledge/collaboration branches, a human decision gate, implementation, monitoring and a feedback return to problem structuring. This is already a DSS feedback model; distinguish it from the proposed *organization-level coevolution* framework rather than calling feedback new.
- **Appraisal:** useful broad positioning source. Its workflow cycle is not a tested account of decades of historical DSS change. Strong historical/organizational causal claims should use primary sources.

## Scope and novelty statements to use or avoid

**Defensible:** “This review integrates organizational information-processing theory with historical DSS and data-dependent decision-system literature to distinguish reinforcing and balancing mechanisms. Its contribution is an evidence-linked mechanism framework, not discovery of reciprocal technology–organization relationships.”

**Avoid:** “Previous DSS reviews ignore feedback”; “this is the first review to connect organizational complexity with data”; “more data inevitably produces more organizational complexity”; “the model establishes the historical cause of every DSS generation”; “85 prior studies prove autonomous DSS create business value.”

A targeted search cannot establish an exhaustive first-in-literature claim. The framework should remain conceptual where any whole-loop link lacks direct longitudinal evidence. A positive polarity means a hypothesized direction under specified conditions, not an unconditional causal coefficient. Delays, modularity, data minimization, standardization, human attention and governance can weaken/reverse some links.
