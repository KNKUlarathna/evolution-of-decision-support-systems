# Organizational implementation evidence

## Sutton2020 — secondary synthesis, clinical DSS

Inspected full article: introduction and system classification, benefits/use cases, pitfalls, maintenance and interoperability, recommendations, conclusion. Publisher HTML headings and PDF provide locators; no source-reported percentage is treated as a pooled effect.

- **Fragmented workflows**: stand-alone DSS can require duplicate documentation and extra sourcing; even integrated systems can disrupt face-to-face work. Supports workload/attention and coordination costs; not proof all DSS increase organizational complexity.
- **Alert fatigue and inappropriate alerts**: high exposure to low-value alerts can produce non-reading and overrides. Supports difference between stored data and attention-facing information; volume of alerts is not identical to raw data volume. Recommendations prioritize consequential alerts.
- **System and content maintenance**: knowledge/rule updates are an ongoing organizational burden. Supports capacity being partly maintenance staffing and ownership, rather than processor speed.
- **Operational impact of poor data quality and incorrect content**: disconnected inventory and medication definitions can generate unsuitable recommendations; workarounds can compromise input data. Supports a reciprocal workflow–data-quality mechanism, but is secondary evidence reported from other studies.
- **Lack of transportability and interoperability**: diverse clinical sources, standards, privacy and connections complicate integration; institutions and mandates matter alongside technical architecture.
- **Benefits and use cases**: successful CDS examples exist; the review does not justify an inevitable complexity spiral. No outcome statistic is generalized here.

## Greenhalgh2017 — framework development and organizational case evidence

Inspected abstract, Methods, all six case summaries, Table 2/domain framework, Discussion and limitations. The study combines prior frameworks with over400 hours observation,165 interviews and200 documents (authors' reported dataset). This is an interpretive design, not a randomized estimate of complexity's effect.

- **Results, Case F: Integrated Case Management Via Data Sharing**: growing emergency-admission needs across older people with multiple needs motivate multidisciplinary coordination; one site deployed an integrated warehouse plus predictive risk modeling and shared care plans. In actual use, identification combined risk stratification with clinical judgment, and plans circulated both through and outside the warehouse. This directly connects organizational interdependence, data integration and real DSS use; it does not show a measured upward time series of data volume or establish causality.
- **Results, Case D: Remote Biomarker Monitoring**: monitoring produces repeated measurements, alerts and possible follow-up work; uptake and interpretations differ, unofficial nurse support can maintain engagement, connectivity excludes some users. Supports redistribution of work and institutional/resource limits, not inevitable improvement from collecting more data.
- **Results, Case B: GPS Tracking**: useful tracking depended on a sociotechnical care network and repeated local adaptation; only3 of11 eligible individuals were using it after18months. Broader care technology example, not a DSS accuracy comparison; illustrates failure of technological determinism.
- **Framework, Domain 7 and Discussion**: multiple domains co-evolve over time; complicated versus complex distinguishes predictable interactions from emergent/unpredictable ones. Supports conditional adaptation and balancing, not treating complexity as a single numeric scalar.
- **Transfer limit**: six UK health/social-care cases; organizational mechanisms are suggestive beyond healthcare, but cross-sector effect sizes and historical generation changes are not established.

## Excluded/context-only candidates

Beede2020 is a promising clinic-deployment study but only its author-page abstract was accessed. Brynjolfsson2016 metadata was verified from Crossref but no full text was assessed. Neither should be counted as an included study or cited to support substantive results in the manuscript.
